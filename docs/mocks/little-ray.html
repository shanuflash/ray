Little Ray

Ray's Little Arc: a small window of its own for a link from another app (with the setting on) or for ⌥⌘N. One page, no sidebar, always a normal (not private) tab. Open in Ray moves the live page, history and all, into the top of Today in Ray's window; closing it throws the page away (its visits stay in History). Several can be open at once. Pick one option per row; the scene combines them. Windows are drawn at about two-thirds size, the bars at full size.

Window chrome

A · Ray's row Ray / Ardia

The pop-up window you picked (B) with what a page needs: one 38 pt row in Ray's sidebar colour, the traffic lights, back and forward, the address centred (click it for the command bar, ⌘L), Copy Link, site controls (extensions, permissions) and Open in Ray. The page is a card 6 pt in. Ardia's Little Ardia has the same pieces.

B · Arc's strip Arc

Arc's measured layout (Cra's notes): a 46 pt strip, one field across it with the address centred and Copy Link and site controls inside its right end, then Open in Ray. No back or forward buttons (swipe, ⌘[ ⌘]). Taller; the most Arc.

C · Quiet

The 38 pt row with only the lights and the address. Copy Link, site controls and Open in Ray fade in while the pointer is over the row (the keys always work). The least chrome; the button you need is hidden until you reach for it.

Size and placement

1 · Centred Ardia

900×640 the first time, then the last size you gave one; never past 80 % of the screen. Centred on the screen the pointer is on; a second one opens 24 pt down and right of the first. Opens with macOS's own window animation.

2 · Grows from the link Arc

Same size rule and spot as 1, but the window grows out of a small box under the pointer to its frame in 0.12 s (Arc's, measured by Cra), so you see where it came from. ⌥⌘N grows from the screen's centre.

3 · Small, by the link

A narrower 560×700 window whose top left sits at the pointer, kept on screen: a quick look beside what you were doing rather than over it. Remembers its size too.

Open in Ray

1 · Icon Ardia

A 28 pt icon button (two arrows out), its tooltip "Open in Ray ⌘O". Smallest; you learn it once.

2 · Labelled Arc

"Open in Ray" with its key, ⌘O, as Arc's "Open in <Space> ⌘O". Always clear what it does. When Spaces land it becomes "Open in <Space>".

3 · Labelled, with where

2 plus Arc's chevron: a menu of where it goes: Today (⌘O), Pinned, Favorites, then each folder. Later the Spaces, as Arc's chevron lists them.

Whichever: the page moves live (no reload, history kept) to the top of Today, becomes the active tab, Ray's window comes forward and the little window goes. Copy Link copies and shows "Copied Current URL", as ⇧⌘C does in Ray's window.

Empty Little Ray (⌥⌘N)

E1 · Bar in the window Arc

The window opens on a blank page with Ray's command bar inside it, a third of the way down (Arc's). Type, Return loads it there. Escape closes the bar and, with nothing loaded, the window.

E2 · Field in the row

The window opens with its own address field editable in the row, suggestions hanging under it, like Chrome's address bar. Nothing floats over the page.

E3 · Bar alone first

⌥⌘N shows only the command bar, centred on the screen like Spotlight, with no window; Return grows a Little Ray out of it with the page. Escape leaves nothing behind.

Questions for you

  1. Default for "Open links from other apps in Little Ray": on (Arc's and Ardia's default) or off (today's behaviour: a Today tab)? And Settings › General (next to the default browser row) or › Tabs?
  2. ⌥⌘N: Ray gives it to New Folder today. Arc gives it to Little Arc and New Folder has no key. Move it to Little Ray and leave New Folder without one?
  3. Open in Ray's key: ⌘O (Arc's and Ardia's; free in Ray) or ⌥⌘O?
  4. Closing: throw the page away (only History keeps it), or also put it in Recently Closed so ⇧⌘T brings it back as a Today tab (Arc keeps closed Little Arcs in its archive)?
